I felt like moving one step closer to the ***real*** website this week.

After finishing the rough flow last week, I started to look back at my wireframe and think about whether it was actually useful for development. At first, my wireframe was mostly just a few boxes showing where things might go. It was enough to explain the basic idea, but not enough to guide the actual building process. So I spent some time developing it a bit further, especially around the homepage and the Sound Room detail page.

The main thing I wanted to make clearer was the relationship between different parts of the website. For example, the Sound Room cards on the homepage should not just be visual blocks. They need to give users enough information to understand what each room is about, then encourage them to click into one. After entering a room, the detail page should continue that experience by becoming a space for listening, reading comments, and joining the discussion. So the wireframe needed to show not only what appears on the page, but also why each part exists.

We also did a small informal user test on the tutorial. Nothing too official, no lab coat, no clipboard. I simply showed the wireframe to a few of our friends and asked them what they thought they could click, where they expected to go, and whether the idea of “Sound Room” made sense without me explaining too much.

The result was useful, but also slightly painful. Some parts that felt obvious to others were not that obvious to me. For example, some of them said that they couldn't understand each sound room properly. However, I do admit that if users could not quickly understand what a Sound Room was from the homepage, then the whole flow became weaker. Furthermore, it illustrates that the homepage needs to explain the concept more clearly before asking users to click further.

So before moving into HTML, I adjusted the wireframe again. I tried to make the Sound Room cards clearer, the entry points more obvious, and the connection between homepage and detail page more logical. I did not want to code a structure that only made sense in my own head.

Once the basic flow felt clear, I started writing the HTML. I thought this part would be smoother because I already had the Figma prototype as a guide.

At first, I did not have to keep stopping and thinking, like “Wait, what am I doing?” I focused first on the overall structure, then moved to the smaller details on each page.

However, one difficulty was translating the wireframe into actual HTML sections. In Figma, everything can sit nicely inside a box, but in code I had to think more carefully about headings, containers, links, and repeated card structures. The Sound Room cards were a good example. No matter what I did, these cards would't stay where I put them.

***Really Naughty.***

I think this is most likely because the cards need to adapt to the screen size, rather than simply staying in one place like headings do. Of course, I managed to solve them all after all.

By the end of the week, I was close to finishing the basic HTMLs. They are still very early drafts, and I still need to review and iterate them with my teammate. So at this time, I would not say the pages are **done**. They are more like a first version that finally exists outside my brain, which is already ***a small achievement***.
