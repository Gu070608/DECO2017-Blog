## This week, we officially entered the development phase.

As for task allocation, since the project is about five or six pages in total, each of us will work on three pages for now. I’ve chosen to take charge of the homepage, the detail page accessible from the homepage, and the user profile page. We’ve agreed that we must have a first draft ready by a deadline sometime next week. We won’t need complex CSS or databases—just the basic content structure of the pages—so we can keep track of our progress and avoid having to rush at the last minute.

So I started from the most basic question: ***what should these pages actually do?***

Before writing any code, I went online to look for references. I looked at some music-related websites and creative community platforms where users can browse posts or discover content. I was not trying to copy their design, but more to understand their logic: How does the homepage introduce the site? How does it lead users to a detail page? What kind of information is shown on this specific page? This helped me realise that the homepage should not just look nice. It needs to work as an entry point, or a ***hook***, guiding users into the rest of the site.

After getting a rough idea, I moved into Figma and made a wireflow and an easy prototype.

***Almost embarrassingly simple.***

Just a few blocks and buttons, but I think it was useful. This was also connected to what we discussed in the tutorial about wireframes and user flow diagrams. I realised that for a user-generated website like ours, a normal sitemap is not enough, because I also need to show what users can do on each page and how they move between browsing, entering a room, listening, and commenting.

I used it to check whether the sections were logical before I started coding: the homepage acts as a discovery space for different Sound Rooms shared by users. Users can browse the rooms, quickly understand their themes, and choose one to enter. After clicking into one, they arrive at a detail page that combines listening and discussion. Users can listen to the music, read forum-style comments, and interact through bullet comments.

At this stage, the prototype was less about visual design and more about asking: ***does this flow make sense?***

There were still some small problems, of course. The main trade-off was simplicity versus ambition. Some sections felt awkward at first, and I had to think about what content should appear on each page without making everything too crowded. I also had to remind myself not to add too many extra functions at this stage. It is very easy to suddenly think, “Oh, maybe I can add this, and this, and this” and then accidentally create hundreds of new problems for myself. I could try to make the pages more complete and impressive from the beginning, but that would probably slow me down and make the structure harder to control.

Instead, I chose to finish a basic thing first: ***not perfect, but clear enough.***
